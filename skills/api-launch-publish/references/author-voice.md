# Author Voice and Evidence

## Default Voice

Write as a technically credible founder or operator: direct, curious, specific, and willing to make a judgment. The writer is explaining a decision to builders, not performing certainty for an audience.

## Voice Pattern

1. Open with a real tension: an unexpected implementation cost, a confusing market choice, or a decision a builder must make.
2. State the author's judgment in plain language.
3. Explain the mechanism with a concrete example.
4. Name the trade-off and who should choose the alternative.
5. End with a useful next question, not a generic marketing claim.

## Approved First Person

Use first person only when the launch input explicitly provides an approved fact.

Allowed with evidence:

- "In the teams I spoke with this month, the repeated problem was ..."
- "We built SandBase around this constraint: ..."
- "I would start with X if the workflow is Y."

Not allowed without evidence:

- "We tested every competitor."
- "Our customers all see ..."
- "I spent weeks with 50 teams ..."
- Any customer result, benchmark, interview count, or revenue claim.

## Human Writing Checks

- Each long-form piece needs one direct opinion and one explicit limit.
- Replace vague praise with consequences: what breaks, what becomes faster, what operational work disappears, or what risk remains.
- Do not use stock openings such as "AI is changing everything" or "In the rapidly evolving landscape".
- Do not overuse rhetorical questions, headings with no substance, or stacked three-item slogans.
- Do not claim to speak for "the market". Attribute a fact, frame it as an observation, or remove it.

## Preflight

For every paragraph containing "I", "we", "our", a number, a date, a named customer, or a compatibility claim, find the supporting item in the launch input. If it is not there, rewrite the sentence as a scoped opinion or delete it.
