# X, LinkedIn, and Discord Playbook

Last updated: 2026-08-23

## Workflow boundary

Codex owns research, source verification, drafting, channel adaptation, and image
briefs. The operator owns final approval and publication. Drafts default to
`needs review`; publication status changes only after operator confirmation and
a live URL or equivalent evidence is recorded.

## Editorial spine

SandBase social content centers on **Agents and the Harness layer**. The working
distinction is:

> Models reason, MCP connects tools, sandboxes isolate execution, and a Harness
> operates the Agent across sessions, permissions, failures, audit, and replay.

This is a positioning lens, not permission to make unsupported comparisons.
Skills, CLI/MCP, and APIs appear when they help explain or run the same Agent
workflow; they do not displace the Agent/Harness narrative.

## Company-account standard

- Lead with one precise, supportable observation—not hype or a product slogan.
- Explain a mechanism, boundary, or practical consequence for builders.
- Use only live product pages, approved articles, repositories, or verified
  release notes as sources.
- Do not invent adoption, benchmarks, customers, quotes, tests, or urgency.
- Avoid generic launches such as “we're excited,” engagement bait, and empty
  trend commentary.
- State a material limitation when the CTA would otherwise mislead.
- Use one CTA. A post should still be useful if the reader never clicks it.
- Do not use fake UI, fake metrics, or decorative AI imagery that implies a
  product capability.

## Channel打法

### X

Default to thoughtful replies in Agent, Harness, sandbox, MCP, permissions, and
runtime discussions. Add one technical distinction and do not attach a product
link unless it directly answers the discussion.

Publish 0–2 original posts per week by default, with a ceiling of 3–5 only when
there are genuinely distinct verified updates. One strong point per post; use a
thread only for a real sequence. Check recent account history before publishing
to avoid repetition.

### LinkedIn

Use 120–250 words to explain what changed, why it matters, two or three practical
implications, and one honest boundary. Favor product decisions, engineering
lessons, and evidence-backed build-in-public notes over short X-style claims.

### Discord

Use 80–160 words. Tell active builders what changed, who can use it, how to try
it, current limitations or status, and where to leave feedback. Discord is an
operational update, not an announcement poster.

## Review gate

Before any original post:

1. Is the source approved and live?
2. Is the opening a useful claim that SandBase can defend?
3. Does the copy match this channel rather than repeat another channel?
4. Are facts, links, availability, and CTA verified?
5. Is the post materially different from recent account content?
6. Is publication more useful than silence or a targeted reply?

If any answer is no, do not publish. Save the draft with `blocked` or `needs
review` status and record the missing evidence.

## Competitive references

Study infrastructure companies for reusable communication patterns: lead with
the reader's problem, explain the mechanism, and disclose constraints. Do not
copy their claims, voice, launch cadence, memes, or benchmark framing without
SandBase evidence.
